## 1. Purpose

Align the CORR9 verification contract with the Owner-selected trusted-host model without weakening the accepted OD-37, OD-38, OD-39, OD-45-A or EFCA scientific and procedural requirements.

The alignment distinguishes three independent properties:

1. Actor-level authorization and confidentiality.
2. Native-history semantic integrity and mode-context consistency.
3. Cryptographically authenticated historical origin.

CORR9 MUST enforce properties 1 and 2 within its defined offline trust model.

Property 3 is NOT established by the current implementation and is NOT required for the presently authorized synthetic-only trusted-host scope.

No claim of authenticated origin may be inferred from a valid SHA-256 event chain alone.

## 3. Trusted-host boundary

### TB-01 — Trusted execution host

The EFCA engine host is the trusted authority responsible for constructing sessions, controlling complete native histories and invoking host persistence and restoration operations.

This is an external execution-boundary assumption. The existence of host-only methods in a Python module does not, by itself, establish operating-system access control, process isolation or caller authentication.

### TB-02 — Actor-facing access

Actors operating through authorized EFCA requester interfaces MUST NOT obtain an operation permitting arbitrary reading, replacement, rewriting or restoration of authoritative complete histories.

Actor-facing operations MUST preserve the established OD-37 disclosure boundary.

### TB-03 — Host persistence

Within the currently authorized workflow, authoritative histories MUST be generated and restored through the trusted engine-host workflow.

Untrusted external history files MUST NOT be promoted into OWNER_PINNED authority solely because they pass internal schema, SHA-256-chain and semantic-replay checks.

### TB-04 — Environment prerequisites

Before CORR9 is integrated into any externally reachable application, that integration must independently demonstrate:

- Isolation of host persistence operations from requester operations.
- Appropriate filesystem and process permissions.
- Prevention of untrusted history replacement or upload-to-restore paths.
- Preservation of the OWNER_PINNED/SIMULATION separation.
- Explicit treatment of compromised-host and privileged-filesystem scenarios.

These are future integration obligations, not claims that the current offline candidate has already proved production isolation.

### INV-02 — Registration entitlement

The mandatory registration order remains:

1. Validate actor identity and CUSTODIAN role.
2. Validate state-independent request inputs.
3. Verify entitlement for the exact tuple.
4. For an entitled actor only, inspect own registration/occupancy.
5. Perform the lawful registration transition.

Denied operations MUST NOT mutate authoritative history or requester-visible state.

### INV-03 — Engine-derived modes

The effective authority mode MUST be derived by the engine.

Explicit simulation construction MUST remain SIMULATION even when its allocation bytes are identical to the Owner-pinned artifact.

Caller-selected modes MUST NOT be accepted as proof of authority.

### INV-04 — Untampered native-history separation

An unchanged SIMULATION-native history MUST NOT restore into an OWNER_PINNED context.

An unchanged OWNER_PINNED-native history MUST NOT restore into a SIMULATION context.

Both refusals MUST be verified even when the underlying allocation bytes and allocation digest are identical.

### INV-05 — Native semantic replay

Live and replay execution MUST validate the same procedural entitlements and engine-derived allocationDigest.

Correctly rehashing an unauthorized registration, a mismatched allocationDigest or an otherwise illegal transition MUST NOT make that event lawful.

### INV-06 — Evidence mode

Evidence records produced by native engine operations MUST carry the correct engine-derived allocationAuthorityMode.

Native SIMULATION issuance MUST NOT claim OWNER_PINNED.

A record's declared mode is procedural context metadata, NOT standalone cryptographic proof of the origin of a subsequently supplied history file.

### INV-07 — Existing procedural safeguards

The CORR5 legacy admission path remains read-only and non-adoptable.

## 5. Explicit non-guarantee — NG-01

The offline EFCA SHA-256 event chain is NOT an independently authenticated attestation of history origin.

A privileged filesystem/process actor capable of replacing an entire persisted chain and recomputing its hashes may create a procedurally consistent substitute history.

A congruent rewrite of GENESIS authority mode and issued evidence metadata may be accepted when the rewritten history matches the receiving host context.

This is a KNOWN, INDEPENDENTLY REPRODUCED LIMITATION under the accepted trusted-host model.

The limitation MUST remain visible in the audit findings and evidence documentation.

No external signature, authenticated checkpoint, hardware trust anchor or equivalent origin-proof mechanism is claimed.

## 6. External evidence acquisition

The origin of retrieved source documents and the authority of EFCA native histories are separate matters.

Obtaining corporate information from official websites, regulatory filings or other trusted document sources does NOT authenticate the origin of an EFCA persisted history.

Retrieved documents are evidence inputs. They do not confer permission to modify host files, select authority modes, issue host commands or assert Owner approval.

Any future retrieval-to-EFCA integration must maintain that separation.

## 8. Revised verdict criteria

**PASS**

The exact unchanged implementation satisfies all in-scope normative invariants INV-01 through INV-08 and RV-01 through RV-10; there are no unaccepted BLOCKING or MAJOR violations within the defined trusted-host offline synthetic scope.

NG-01 remains expressly disclosed as an Owner-risk-accepted limitation.

A revised PASS does not assert authenticated history origin.

**FAIL**

A reproducible violation of an in-scope invariant exists, including unauthorized first-writer registration, a requester-facing history-forgery path, simulation-to-Owner authority escalation through permitted actor operations, or regression of prior safeguards.

**HOLD**

The necessary authority, repository, test environment, or evidence is unavailable to establish required in-scope properties.

Do not substitute a declaration of trusted-host separation for an actual verification of the available requester-facing code paths.
