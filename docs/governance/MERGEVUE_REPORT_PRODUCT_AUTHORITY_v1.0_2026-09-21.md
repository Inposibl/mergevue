# 1. GOVERNING REPORT PRINCIPLE

MergeVue is an almost fully automated B2B SaaS decision-support product, not consulting hidden behind software.

The governing product principle is:

> **THE SYSTEM DOES THE ANALYSIS. HUMAN EXPERT TIME INCREASES ONLY WHEN THE VALUE / UNCERTAINTY OF THE DECISION JUSTIFIES ADDITIONAL ASSURANCE.**

Higher price primarily means deeper admissible evidence, harder uncertainty resolution, stronger assurance, and human adjudication where actually necessary.

Higher price does **not** mean:
- arbitrary page unlocks;
- mandatory additional experts;
- stronger claims merely because the client paid more;
- a parallel analyst-report universe;
- guaranteed adverse findings;
- guaranteed formal forecast;
- guaranteed determinate answer.

A visible **What changed** layer explains what strengthened, weakened, contradicted, resolved, or remained unresolved relative to the previous released version.

# 10. THEORY-FIRST RETRO-AUDIT CORRECTIONS TO SECTIONS 7–10, 17–18

These decisions close the analytical ambiguities exposed by Decision 76.

## 10.1 Section 8 / Block 14 — Economic Exposure and Value Dependency

Economic exposure is a **causal value-dependency map**, not a financial forecast.

A lawful projection may explain:
- which value thesis / synergy assumption / operating dependency is exposed;
- through which supported organizational mechanism;
- which observable signals matter;
- which falsifier could weaken/change the view.

Do not infer:

`ECS → dollar loss`

Do not invent:
- expected loss;
- ROI;
- EV discount;
- fabricated monetary precision.

A monetary figure may appear only when it comes from a separately admissible financial input/model and the linkage to the organizational mechanism is established.

## 10.2 Section 17 — Role Dependency vs Person Dependency

Role Dependency and Person Dependency are distinct.

MergeVue may establish that a **role** is critical without claiming that a named individual is a specific person-risk.

A named-person conclusion requires a separate lawful chain:

`critical role`
→ `named person actually occupies/controls that dependency`
→ `admissible person-specific evidence`
→ `relevant behavioral/decision signal`
→ `execution consequence`

If the chain is incomplete, do not infer a person-specific risk.

42Q may deepen person-specific analysis but may not invent it.

## 10.3 Sections 9 and 18 — Action Traceability

Actions must not be generic M&A best practices.

Each lawful action must trace through:

`evidence`
→ `Environment/mechanism interpretation`
→ `specific friction/dependency`
→ `control objective`
→ `recommended action`
→ `observable success/failure signal`

Report grouping may distinguish:
- Diligence Action;
- Control Action;
- Integration Action;

but these are presentation groupings unless separately established upstream.

A valid recommendation may be to **defer or avoid an irreversible action** until a specified signal/evidence condition is met.

## 10.4 Section 7 — Timing

Timing claims require upstream support.

Lawful chain:

`mechanism`
→ `prerequisite / trigger`
→ `expected observable transition`
→ `timing basis`
→ `observation window`
→ `falsifier`

If the methodology supports only causal sequence, use stage-/trigger-based sequencing.

Day 30 / Day 60 / Day 100 may be operational observation checkpoints. They are not proof that an event must occur at that time.

Do not fabricate calendar precision.

## 10.5 Section 10 — Decision Gap

Decision Gap means:

> **unresolved decision-relevant uncertainty**

It does **not** mean:
- missing pages;
- missing paid access;
- generic lack of data;
- a sales opportunity.

Lawful logic:

`client decision question`
→ `what MergeVue can currently support`
→ `material unresolved alternative / uncertainty`
→ `why it could change the decision/control`
→ `what exact evidence/adjudication/assurance could discriminate it`
→ `what becomes decidable if closed`

Rules:
- no material decision consequence → no Decision Gap;
- `NOT DETERMINABLE` may be final;
- Decision Gap is separate from paywall/tier qualification;
- zero Decision Gap is valid;
- a display ceiling never creates a Decision Gap.

# 11. CLAIM / NARRATIVE PROJECTION RULES — DECISIONS 77–78

## 11.1 Decision 77 — Claim Traceability Contract

Every material analytical statement in a report must be traceable to the existing upstream analytical result that authorizes it.

The report must preserve, as applicable:
- semantic proposition;
- analytical state;
- scope;
- evidence/provenance;
- material alternative / contradiction;
- falsifier;
- assurance / version.

This is a **report traceability requirement**. It does not by itself require a new methodology-level `CanonicalClaimObject` entity.

Analytical state and product display state are distinct.

For example, an internal lawful result may be stronger than the Anonymous FREE display ceiling, while the FREE client surface remains `LIMITED`.

The report LLM may never upgrade a claim's analytical state or certainty.

## 11.2 Decision 78 — Narrative Roll-Up

Executive summaries, section summaries, and board-level narrative may not invent a new analytical conclusion from a general impression of lower-level results.

A summary may:
- compress existing conclusions without adding meaning; or
- communicate a new conclusion only if that conclusion already exists upstream as an authorized analytical result.

Do not create a parallel methodology-level `DerivedClaim` merely for report convenience.

Summary selection should reflect:
- decision relevance;
- materiality;
- existing confidence/assurance;
- unresolved uncertainty;
- actionability.

Do not select or strengthen claims merely for rhetorical drama.

Material conflict must not be averaged away.

# 12. CONTRADICTION RULES — DECISIONS 79–80

## 12.1 Material unresolved contradictions

If an unresolved contradiction could materially change:
- Environment interpretation;
- friction/dependency;
- economic exposure;
- Decision Gap;
- recommended action;

it must be visible in the main report.

Non-material/contextual contradictions may remain in the Evidence Index.

Expert handling may resolve contradictions only through admissible evidence and existing method rules.

## 12.2 Resolved contradictions remain in history

A decision-material contradiction that is lawfully resolved must not disappear without trace.

The current version should preserve a compact resolution history:
- current conclusion;
- previously unresolved contradiction;
- admissible evidence/adjudication basis that resolved it;
- residual uncertainty, if any.

Unresolved material contradictions remain visible.

Non-material contradiction history may remain in the Evidence Index.

# 13. TEMPORAL / VERSION / PROVENANCE RULES — DECISIONS 81, 104, 106–107

## 13.1 Report as-of boundary

Each released report has an explicit temporal knowledge boundary:
- Report as-of / Evidence through;
- release timing.

A released report is valid relative to the evidence state it actually analyzed.

Material new evidence may justify review and a new immutable version.

New evidence alone does not prove the prior conclusion wrong.

## 13.2 PRE-T0 / later evidence separation

Later/post-T0 evidence may update the current interpretation and justify a new version.

It must not retroactively rewrite what was lawfully known or inferred from the earlier PRE-T0/evidence state.

A new version should distinguish:
- original pre-deal view;
- later evidence;
- effect on current interpretation.

## 13.3 Methodology binding

Each released report must be bound to the governing methodology/math version used to generate it.

A later method change does not silently reinterpret the old report.

Evidence-driven reruns and methodology-driven reruns must remain distinguishable.

## 13.4 Standing rule for methodology changes

For any future methodology/governing-rule change:

> **Only reports whose material conclusions actually depend on the changed governing rule require review/rerun. There is no automatic portfolio-wide rerun or update.**

Do not re-ask the Owner this question unless the Owner explicitly changes this standing rule.

# 14. EVIDENCE ACCOUNTABILITY — DECISIONS 82, 84, 86, 88, 103

## 14.1 Evidence Basis + Evidence Index

Material conclusions should show a concise client-safe **Evidence Basis** in the main report.

A deeper `Why this conclusion? / Evidence Index` may expose, subject to access rights:
- support;
- counterevidence;
- contradictions;
- provenance;
- dates;
- lawful falsifier / limitation.

Black Box protects proprietary methodology, not evidence accountability.

Do not expose by default:
- internal codes;
- proprietary mappings;
- weights;
- thresholds;
- derivations;
- prompt/model internals;
- raw hashes / byte offsets / ingestion IDs unless separately justified.

## 14.2 Private evidence does not automatically override public evidence

Private/internal evidence has no automatic supremacy merely because it is internal or paid.

Its weight and ability to resolve contradictions are governed by the existing evidence methodology/source-role rules.

Paid/private scope provides deeper evidence access and analysis, not client-controlled truth.

## 14.3 Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence

By default:

`not observed ≠ absent`

A statement such as `No material conflict identified` requires sufficient coverage and lawful negative inference under existing methodology.

Where coverage is insufficient, preserve existing gap / uncertainty / `NOT DETERMINABLE` semantics.

Do not create a new negative-evidence state.

## 14.4 No silent scope expansion

Claim scope may be no broader than the organizational/business-unit/function/geography/person/domain scope actually supported by evidence and existing methodology.

Paid tiers do not automatically broaden analytical scope.

## 14.5 Deal context is not analytical evidence

Deal/company context may be shown for orientation.

Examples:
- parties;
- transaction structure;
- provenance-backed deal value;
- sector;
- announced/close timing;
- geography.

Context does not become analytical evidence unless existing methodology lawfully links it to the relevant mechanism/tree semantics.

Do not infer generic shortcuts such as:
- large deal → high integration risk;
- technology company → innovative Environment;
- cross-border → cultural collision.

# 15. PAID SCOPE / RESULT INTEGRITY — DECISIONS 83, 116–121

## 15.1 Paid scope does not guarantee a determinate or adverse result

A paid tier guarantees that the authorized scope and assurance depth were performed.

It does **not** guarantee:
- a determinate conclusion;
- a red flag;
- an adverse result;
- a formal forecast.

Paid blocks remain visible and honestly show lawful outcomes such as:
- `NOT DETERMINABLE`;
- no material conflict/dependency found;
- other existing upstream states.

## 15.2 Block 19 does not require a forecast

A complete $30k Block 19 may contain:
- High-Assurance Review;
- Audit / Release Record.

Forecast Lock appears only when the governing methodology determines that an eligible formal forecast exists.

The tier does not create forecast eligibility.

## 15.3 Not performed ≠ not determinable

An optional lane not authorized/performed must not be presented as `NOT DETERMINABLE`.

`NOT DETERMINABLE` is reserved for analysis actually performed but unresolved.

If an optional lane was not performed:
- omit that optional submodule; or
- disclose in scope/assurance information that it was outside performed scope.

## 15.4 Paid upgrade must show what was actually added

Each paid upgrade must show the real analytical/evidence/assurance delta relative to the prior released version.

The client should be able to see, as applicable:
- additional evidence reviewed;
- contradictions resolved/discovered;
- findings changed/unchanged;
- additional assurance;
- optional analysis actually performed.

If the conclusion is unchanged, say so.

Do not change a conclusion merely to justify the upgrade.

## 15.5 Decision 119 — Purchase is client-initiated

A FREE display ceiling does not create a Decision Gap.

MergeVue must not push purchase with:

> “Pay and we will show you what we already found.”

The decision to purchase belongs to the client.

Paid scope is justified only by real additional analytical/evidence/assurance work.

At the same time, MergeVue does not reveal content outside the applicable FREE entitlement.

## 15.6 Decision 120 — Block 11 informs, it does not sell

Block 11 may explain:
- what remains unresolved;
- what additional work/evidence/assurance would be required;
- which available scope covers that work;
- transparent scope names/prices where appropriate.

It must not:
- recommend that the client buy;
- choose a paid tier for the client;
- use urgency/FOMO;
- use default upsells;
- use `best value` framing;
- sell access to a hidden already-known result.

Lawful outcomes include:
- Continue Free;
- Additional Analysis Available;
- No Additional Analysis Currently Justified.

## 15.7 Decision 121 — Paid scope visibility

Existing Anonymous FREE `LIMITED` mechanics for Blocks 7–10 remain exactly as accepted.

These are not paid teasers.

Paid extensions 13–19 may be neutrally described before purchase in terms of **what additional analysis they perform**.

Do not tease, blur, or reveal deal-specific paid findings before the relevant paid scope is actually performed/released.

Pre-purchase scope description may state **what will be investigated**, not **what MergeVue has already found** in the current deal.

# 16. EXPERT / ASSURANCE BOUNDARY — DECISIONS 85, 110

## 16.1 Expert adjudication

Expert adjudication operates only inside the existing evidence and methodology envelope.

An expert may:
- inspect evidence;
- resolve contradictions where the method permits;
- choose among already-supported alternatives where adjudication is lawful;
- reduce overconfidence;
- preserve `NOT DETERMINABLE`;
- trigger a separate correction/methodology track when a defect is found.

An expert may not:
- freely override Environment interpretation;
- ignore material alternatives without evidence basis;
- raise certainty from intuition;
- invent a new mechanism;
- rewrite conclusions to make the report more useful.

## 16.2 Client-safe assurance basis

The report should expose a client-safe assurance basis for material conclusions at a high level, such as:
- public/structured evidence only;
- reviewed against private evidence;
- expert adjudication;
- high-assurance review.

Do not expose:
- internal agent/model names;
- orchestration paths;
- validator names;
- internal model routing.

Assurance basis explains the review path. It does not itself change the analytical conclusion.

# 17. DEAL-DECISION BOUNDARY / POSITIVE FINDINGS / PAIR / ECS / SYNTHETIC METRICS — DECISIONS 87, 89–92, 96–97

## 17.0 Decision 89 — No autonomous whole-transaction verdict

Even the **$30k Investment Committee Analysis** remains decision support inside MergeVue's evidenced domain.

It must not issue an autonomous whole-transaction:
- BUY / NO-BUY;
- PROCEED / STOP;
- equivalent cross-domain deal verdict.

MergeVue may state transaction implications, material conditions, dependencies, unresolved uncertainties, and controls that follow from its own evidenced analytical domain.

It may not convert those findings into a universal acquisition/investment decision that would require valuation, legal, tax, commercial, financing, or other authorities outside MergeVue.

Do not create a Deal Recommendation Score, Proceed State, or equivalent report-layer entity.

## 17.1 Positive findings

Reports must preserve evidence-backed positive/reinforcing findings, not only risks.

Positive findings require the same theory-first discipline as adverse findings.

Do not invent strengths merely for balance.

A positive mechanism-level finding does not imply overall deal success.

## 17.2 No forced Acquirer/Target symmetry

Visual structure may be symmetrical for comparison.

Evidence depth, uncertainty, scope, and analytical state must remain side-specific.

Strong evidence on one side must not mask weak evidence on the other.

## 17.3 No pair certainty invented by report layer

Pair-level conclusion, including ECS interpretation, may be shown only to the extent lawfully established upstream.

The report may not create a collision thesis when one side remains unresolved.

Strong evidence on one side does not compensate for unresolved Environment determination on the other.

## 17.4 ECS hierarchy

ECS remains an important visible structural compatibility measure when lawfully supported.

It must not dominate the report or turn MergeVue into a one-number product.

The report hierarchy should center on:
- principal finding;
- underlying mechanism;
- decision implication;
- material uncertainty.

If ECS is not lawfully determinable, do not invent a substitute score.

## 17.5 No synthetic scores or bands

The report layer may show:
1. factual inputs with provenance; or
2. lawful outputs already defined by governing methodology/math.

Do not create:
- Risk Score;
- Severity Index;
- Exposure Score;
- Confidence %;
- synthetic 0–100 measures;
- synthetic HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW bands.

Historical visual references do not create numeric authority.

## 17.6 No synthetic Top-Risk ranking

Do not create `Top Risks`, `#1 Risk`, `Most Important Risk`, or similar ordering unless that ordering is already produced lawfully upstream.

Visual position, rhetoric, or LLM judgment must not silently become a prioritization rule.

# 18. FIRST-PAGE / PRESENTATION EPISTEMICS — DECISIONS 93–95, 98–100, 102

## 18.1 Cover must not force an action

The stable first-page element is **immediate decision implication**, not a mandatory `First Integration Control Move`.

If a lawful control action exists, it may be shown.

If only monitoring/deferment is supported, show that.

If no lawful implication exists, do not manufacture one.

## 18.2 Fact / interpretation / forecast / action separation

Client-facing projection must preserve the distinction between:
- evidence / observed fact;
- analytical interpretation;
- forward-looking implication / forecast;
- recommendation / control action.

Forecast or recommendation must never be presented as observed fact.

Conditional language must not be upgraded to certainty in Executive Summary.

## 18.3 Compression must not remove material uncertainty

Board Briefs, IC Briefs, one-pagers, PDF/email summaries, and PPTX snapshots may reduce detail.

They may not remove decision-material:
- uncertainty;
- contradiction;
- scope limitation;
- `NOT DETERMINABLE`;
- key condition/falsifier;
- other limitation that changes decision meaning.

Compression must not increase apparent certainty or broaden scope.

## 18.4 Cross-projection semantic consistency

The same analytical conclusion must preserve the same:
- scope;
- uncertainty;
- semantic meaning

wherever it appears.

Only wording, compression, and level of detail may change.

If the underlying conclusion changes, update the new report version and its dependent projections consistently.

## 18.5 Client-visible falsifier

For decision-material conclusions, the main report should show a client-readable:

**What would change this view?**

only when a lawful falsifier already exists upstream.

The report may not invent a falsifier.

## 18.6 Material alternative visibility

A strongest alternative that remains materially plausible and could change transaction implication or control must be visible in the main report.

If it would not change decision meaning, it may remain in the Evidence Index.

The report may state which interpretation is better supported but may not hide a decision-material surviving alternative.

## 18.7 Causal language boundary

Report causal language may be no stronger than the causal mechanism and evidence established upstream.

Keep distinct:
- observed association/pattern;
- supported mechanism;
- forward-looking implication.

A downstream economic statement may not be more causally certain than the organizational mechanism supporting it.

# 19. HISTORICAL / FORECAST ACCOUNTABILITY — DECISIONS 101, 111–115

## 19.1 Historical analogues

Historical cases and benchmarks may provide contextual comparison only within the existing methodology.

They do not replace current-deal evidence and must not become an independent basis for current forecast.

Post-outcome knowledge from historical cases must not be used as a shortcut to current-deal prediction.

Do not invent Analogue Scores or Similarity Indexes.

## 19.2 Not every forward-looking statement is a formal forecast

Watchpoints, timing implications, and recommendations are not formal forecasts merely because they look forward.

Forecast lock/seal and later verification apply only to forecasts that existing governing methodology explicitly permits as eligible formal forecasts.

## 19.3 Exact sealed forecast verification

Formal forecast verification compares observed outcome only against the exact sealed pre-outcome forecast as originally stated, including its original:
- scope;
- time window;
- mechanism;
- falsifier.

Do not redefine forecast meaning post hoc.

## 19.4 Misses remain visible

Verified misses, falsifications, and partial matches remain client-visible in the linked verification/history layer alongside successful forecasts.

No selective reporting or cherry-picking.

## 19.5 No synthetic overall accuracy percentage

Do not show aggregate:
- MergeVue accuracy %;
- hit rate;
- validated-at-X%;
- predictive-performance score

unless the governing calibration framework formally defines the metric, denominator, population, coverage, abstention handling, partial-match treatment, and limitations.

## 19.6 Validation claims stay inside realized coverage

Validation/generalization claims must remain strictly inside empirically realized coverage.

Do not generalize validation across untested:
- Environments;
- roles;
- mechanisms;
- evidence routes;
- time horizons;
- transaction types;
- deal classes.

Methodological support and empirical validation remain distinct.

# 20. GOVERNANCE / ACCESS / CHALLENGE — DECISIONS 105, 108–109, 122

## 20.1 Action owner

A report may identify an accountable owner/role for a control/integration action only when that responsibility is established by admissible evidence, client governance, or an existing upstream control output.

Do not invent:
- Integration Lead;
- Deal Sponsor;
- governance body;
- other owner

merely because a template expects one.

If responsibility is not established:
- omit the owner field; or
- state that the client must assign the responsible role.

## 20.2 Redaction does not change meaning

Recipient permissions may hide restricted evidence.

They may not strengthen, sanitize, or otherwise change the analytical meaning of a claim.

Material uncertainty, contradiction, scope limitation, and qualification must remain visible in recipient-safe projections.

If a claim cannot be shown without violating access restrictions, use a more bounded formulation or omit it rather than create false certainty.

## 20.3 Client challenge is not client override

A client, CEO, adviser, or authorized participant may challenge a released conclusion and trigger review.

Disagreement alone does not authorize a change.

A report changes only when review establishes a lawful basis such as:
- new admissible evidence;
- factual/provenance error;
- scope error;
- incorrect application of existing methodology;
- proven runtime/report defect;
- another existing correction basis.

Client preference is not analytical authority.

## 20.4 Secondary-use audit

The bounded secondary-use audit for client data usage/training/publication/track-record purposes remains a separate Deal Workspace / governance surface.

It is not part of the canonical analytical report.

Section 12 / Evidence Index explains why analytical conclusions exist.

Secondary-use audit explains how client data was actually used outside the primary service-delivery purpose.
