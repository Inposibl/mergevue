# Owner-Accepted Environment Card 8 — STP/STJ

**Status:** Owner-supplied nine-field card integrated in the CORR2 frozen set. Not independently verified or governance-bound by this act.

**Direct semantic authority:** `FINAL_OWNER_AUTHORITY_RECORD_E9_CORR2.md` §8.

## 1. CORE LOGIC

`STP/STJ` is an Environment of organised tactical survival.

It combines:
- external tactical freedom/adaptability toward unstable, restrictive or hostile conditions;
- strict internal order inside the group, based on hierarchy, role discipline, binding internal rules and recognised authority.

Governing rule:
**External rules may be bypassed, broken or used situationally; internal rules of the group must be obeyed.**

Structural shorthand:
`hostile / restrictive outside world → tactical freedom toward outsiders → rigid code and hierarchy among insiders`

More compact:
`external tactical flexibility → internal discipline / hierarchy / loyalty`

Typical examples may include organised criminal groups, prison hierarchies, smuggling networks, guerrilla or pirate groups, and legal businesses in unstable/high-risk/institutionally weak settings.
Examples are NOT classification criteria.

Nearest contrast with STJ/STP:
- STP/STJ: adapt outside → organise inside.
- STJ/STP: establish dominance → redistribute resources accordingly.

## 2. RESOURCE RELATION

STP/STJ does not primarily create resources.
It gains resources from external environment through tactical adaptation:
- identifying opportunities;
- using weak points in external systems;
- informal arrangements;
- alternative routes;
- flexible action where external rules do not protect group interests.

Flow:
`external resource opportunities → tactical acquisition / redistribution → controlled access inside the group → hierarchical retention and distribution`

Inside-group access depends on usefulness, adaptability, role, reliability, loyalty to internal structure, and recognition of hierarchy.

Once resources enter the group, access becomes role-based and controlled.

Distinctive human resource:
**adapting person** = actor who can read the actual situation, adjust quickly, negotiate, organise, move resources through constraints, protect resources and remain effective under changing conditions.

The actor receives protected access to group resource flow in exchange for helping acquire, redistribute, protect and manage resources according to internal rules.

Legacy v1.7 word “unfair” is NOT a current normative criterion.
Current meaning:
redistribution is not governed primarily by impersonal external rules, but by tactical interests and internal hierarchy of the group.

Registry:
`tactical access to external resources → recruitment and retention of adapting people within a protected hierarchical resource network`

## 3. POWER / CONTROL

Power = strict internal hierarchy with delegated operational control.

Four components:

1. Formal/recognised hierarchy determines who has the final word and whose orders are binding.
2. Personal loyalty/reliability determines who is trusted with sensitive information, resources, autonomy and access to the inner circle.
3. Operational competence determines how much tactical freedom is delegated in a specific operation.
4. Control of resources and information makes authority enforceable.

Normal conflict rule:
competent subordinate may determine **how** a task is executed;
higher-ranking actor retains authority over **what** must be done and final decision.

Operational competence may challenge rank, but does not permanently replace rank.
If leader repeatedly fails to protect group, loses resources, makes damaging decisions or threatens survival, authority may erode.
A more competent actor may replace them, but stability returns when superiority becomes a new recognised hierarchical position.

Registry:
`power = recognised hierarchy + personal loyalty + delegated tactical competence + controlled access to resources and information`

Compact:
`hierarchy decides who commands; competence decides delegated freedom; loyalty decides who is trusted; resource/information control makes authority enforceable`

## 4. NECESSARY FEATURES

Exactly four genuinely necessary features:

1. **External adaptive pressure**
   Outside environment requires continuous situational reading/adaptation.
   Literal war/crisis/threat is NOT required.

2. **Tactical freedom outward**
   Participants can vary tactics according to circumstances.
   If external action becomes mainly stable/procedural/predetermined, STP component is lost.

3. **Recognised internal command order**
   There is a real system of authority: participants know who may make final decision, who answers to whom and which internal rules are binding.
   Formal bureaucracy is NOT required.

4. **Insider / outsider rule asymmetry**
   Rules for insiders differ from rules for external interaction.
   Tactical adaptation is permitted outward; internal order is binding inward.

Minimum structural formula:
`external adaptive pressure + tactical freedom outward + recognised internal order + insider/outsider rule asymmetry`

Compact:
`STP/STJ exists when tactical freedom outside is made possible by enforceable order inside`

Not necessary:
literal external threat; personal loyalty to one leader; formal bureaucracy; complex controlled-resource architecture.

Role discipline is necessary functionally, but roles need not be formal.

## 5. TYPICAL BUT NOT REQUIRED FEATURES

Typical mature manifestations:
- personal loyalty / proven reliability;
- secrecy / restricted information;
- closed information channels;
- resource gatekeeping;
- informal deals / negotiated exceptions;
- sanctions for betrayal or serious internal-code violations;
- high value of proven insiders;
- physical-risk / crisis competence;
- crisis conditions.

Criminal, grey-market, military or other high-risk settings are examples only.

Not intrinsic:
- corruption;
- family/clan organisation.

No feature above is mandatory by itself.

## 6. DIFFERENCE FROM NEAREST ENVIRONMENTS

### vs STJ/STP
Primary distinction: adaptation vs dominance.

STP/STJ:
`adapt outside → organise inside`

STJ/STP:
`establish dominance → redistribute resources accordingly`

Test:
if hierarchy mainly coordinates adaptive action → STP/STJ.
If hierarchy itself determines who may take/hold resources → STJ/STP.

### vs SFP/SFJ
Primary distinction: tactical effectiveness vs emotional/sensory engagement.

STP/STJ:
external flexibility exists for tactical effectiveness.

SFP/SFJ:
external engagement/freedom works through desire, pleasure and emotional-sensory involvement.

### vs NT/STJ
Primary distinction: adaptation to reality vs improvement of reality through knowledge.

STP/STJ asks:
“What works here and now in these conditions?”

NT/STJ asks:
“How do we build a better system/method/technology?”

Compact:
- STP/STJ = adapt to the situation without surrendering internal order.
- STJ/STP = control the situation through dominance.
- SFP/SFJ = control behaviour through sensory/emotional engagement.
- NT/STJ = improve the situation through superior systems and technology.

## 7. WHAT MUST NOT BE USED AS A CRITERION

Do NOT infer STP/STJ from any of the following alone:
- criminality;
- illegality;
- secrecy;
- corruption;
- personal loyalty;
- formal hierarchy;
- crisis;
- physical danger;
- informal deals;
- rule-breaking;
- insider language;
- resource gatekeeping;
- strong sanctions;
- closed-group identity;
- opportunism;
- fast decision-making.

Explicitly prohibit:
`criminal + secretive + hierarchical + loyal = STP/STJ`

Required structure:
`external adaptive pressure + tactical flexibility toward outside + recognised enforceable internal order + insider/outsider rule asymmetry`

Diagnostic semantic question:
Does internal discipline make high external tactical flexibility/adaptation possible?

## 8. DOCUMENTARY OBSERVABILITY

Documentary support should come from coincidence of three layers:
`external tactical adaptability ↔ strict internal order ↔ insider/outsider asymmetry`

Potential evidence for external adaptation:
- exception procedures;
- emergency/contingency playbooks;
- authority to change suppliers/routes/channels/intermediaries quickly;
- non-standard transaction permissions;
- broad discretion in selected operational roles;
- escalation rules that permit deviation;
- multiple situational methods for the same objective.

Potential evidence for internal order:
- chain of command;
- delegation-of-authority matrix;
- approval thresholds;
- role-specific access rights;
- escalation paths;
- decision-right rules;
- internal sanctions;
- layered authority;
- central control over critical resources/information.

Potential evidence for insider/outsider asymmetry:
- different procedures/disclosure rules for trusted/internal vs external parties;
- privileged partner/vendor lists;
- closed channels;
- different approval standards;
- sponsorship/referral requirements;
- special access for trusted participants;
- externally negotiable rules vs internally non-negotiable rules.

Strongest documentary pattern:
`broad external operational discretion + narrow internal approval/control + different treatment of trusted insiders and outsiders`

Low-observability elements:
- actual tactical behaviour;
- real vs formal hierarchy;
- personal loyalty;
- trusted-insider status;
- informal sanctions;
- lived “our people” boundary;
- informal information flows;
- real reason for internal discipline.

Documents are strongest for STJ layer, weaker for lived STP dynamics.

## 9. MINIMUM SUFFICIENT MEANING

**`STP/STJ is an Environment where external tactical flexibility is sustained by enforceable internal order and recognised hierarchy.`**
